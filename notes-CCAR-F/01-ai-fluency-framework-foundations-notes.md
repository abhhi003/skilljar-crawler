## 1. Big Picture

- AI Fluency = the ability to collaborate with AI in ways that are effective, efficient,
  ethical, and safe.
- The course is about human-AI collaboration, not about AI as a technology. It aims at a
  lasting framework, not temporary tips and tricks.
- Three ways people engage with AI: Automation, Augmentation, Agency.
- Four core competencies (the "4Ds"): Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence.
- The 4Ds apply across all three ways of engaging with AI.
- Delegation, Description and Discernment mainly serve effectiveness and efficiency.
  Diligence addresses the ethical and safety side.
- Description and Discernment are two halves of one conversation and form a continuous
  feedback loop.
- The framework is designed to stay relevant as AI evolves. Fluency develops through
  practice, not overnight mastery.
- Each competency has three components (named in the lessons below).

```
                    AI FLUENCY (effective, efficient, ethical, safe)

   Ways of engaging with AI:  AUTOMATION | AUGMENTATION | AGENCY
                                  (4Ds apply across all three)

   +-------------+      +-------------+      +--------------+      +-----------+
   | DELEGATION  | ---> | DESCRIPTION | <--> | DISCERNMENT  | ---> | DILIGENCE |
   | what to do  |      | communicate |      | evaluate AI  |      | responsible|
   | with AI vs  |      | clearly to  |      | outputs and  |      | use, own   |
   | yourself    |      | the AI      |      | behavior     |      | the result |
   +-------------+      +-------------+      +--------------+      +-----------+
   Problem Aware.       Product Desc.        Product Disc.         Creation Dil.
   Platform Aware.      Process Desc.        Process Disc.         Transparency Dil.
   Task Delegation      Performance Desc.    Performance Disc.     Deployment Dil.

                  Description <---- feedback loop ----> Discernment
```

### Lesson 3: The 4D Framework (video 5 min; "Lesson 2B")

Objective: explain the basic AI Fluency Framework and its core 4Ds.

Key ideas (named exactly as the course lists them)
- Delegation: thoughtfully deciding what work to do with AI vs. doing yourself.
- Description: communicating clearly with AI systems.
- Discernment: evaluating AI outputs and behavior with a critical eye.
- Diligence: ensuring you interact with AI responsibly.
- The competencies work together across the different ways of engaging with AI, and
  developing them prepares you for whatever AI evolution brings next.
- Key takeaways:
  - Automation: AI executes specific tasks based on your instructions.
  - Augmentation: you and AI collaborate as creative thinking and task execution
    partners.
  - Agency: you guide AI to work independently on your behalf, shaping its knowledge and
    behavior rather than specific actions.
  - The 4Ds: Delegation (deciding what work to do with AI vs. yourself), Description

    critically), Diligence (ensuring responsible AI collaboration).
  - Competencies apply across all three ways of working with AI.

Rules and traps
- The 4Ds are not tied to one mode: they apply to Automation, Augmentation and Agency
  alike.

### Lesson 5: Capabilities & Limitations (Deep Dive, video 7 min; "Lesson 3B")

Objective: identify major capabilities and limitations of current generative AI.

Key ideas (key takeaways as listed)
- Generative AI creates new content (text, images, code) rather than just analyzing
  existing data.
- LLMs were made possible by three developments:
  - Algorithmic and architectural breakthroughs (especially the transformer
    architecture).
  - Vast amounts of digital training data.
  - Dramatic increases in computational power.
- Two learning stages:
  - Pre-training: analyzing patterns across billions of examples.
  - Fine-tuning: learning to follow instructions and provide helpful responses.
- Current capabilities: versatility across tasks (language tasks; switching between
  diverse tasks without additional training), conversational awareness / maintaining
  conversational flow, and the ability to connect with external tools.
- Current limitations: knowledge cutoff dates, potential for hallucinations (factually
  incorrect outputs), context window constraints, and challenges with complex reasoning.
- The field is evolving rapidly.
- The most effective applications combine human and AI strengths, with humans providing
  critical thinking, judgment, creativity, and ethical oversight.

Rules and traps
- Limitations to watch: knowledge cutoff, hallucination, context window, reasoning.
- Do not treat limitations as fixed: the course stresses the field evolves rapidly.

Reflection: see Section 3.

### Lesson 6: A Closer Look at Delegation (video 6 min; "Lesson 4")

Objectives: understand Delegation and its three components; recognize when and how to
delegate to AI; develop awareness of task, platform, and mode considerations.

Key ideas
- Delegation is the first core competency. It is about thoughtful decisions on what work
  to do yourself, what to do together with AI, or what to let AI handle independently,
  and how to distribute those tasks.
- Three components (named exactly):
  - Problem Awareness: understanding your goals and the work involved to achieve it
    (clearly understanding goals and the nature of the work before involving AI).
  - Platform Awareness: knowing what different AI systems can do (their capabilities and
    limitations).
  - Task Delegation: strategically dividing work between you and AI, leveraging the
    strengths of each.
- Effective delegation requires both domain expertise and an understanding of AI
  capabilities.
- The goal is not to automate everything but to create the most effective human-AI
  partnership for any given task or goal.

Rules and traps
- Understand the problem before involving AI (Problem Awareness comes first).
- Delegation is not "automate everything" (see certificate quiz Q1 distractor).
- Expertise in your field AND knowledge of AI are both required.

Exercise: Analyze tasks with an AI assistant (see Section 3).

### Lesson 7: Project Planning and Delegation (hands-on, ~20 min)

Objective: apply Delegation to a practical multi-step project used through the rest of
the course.

Key ideas
- Step 1, choose a project that is:
  - Substantial enough to involve multiple types of tasks.
  - Manageable enough to complete within about 1 hour of work.
  - Something you are genuinely interested in creating or accomplishing.
- Suggested project categories listed by the course:
  - Communication: compelling presentation; post(s) explaining a complex topic to a
    general audience; proposal or pitch; personal or professional bio with supporting
    materials.
  - Research: summarize state of an emerging technology or trend; analyze a dataset for
    patterns and recommendations; compare products/services/approaches and recommend;
    investigate a historical event and present findings engagingly.
  - Creative: outline a short story with developed characters and plot; design a simple
    website structure with content for key pages; develop a product/service/experience
    concept.
  - Learning: structured learning plan for a skill; resource collection on a topic;
    tutorial or guide for a process you understand; study materials for a topic.
- Step 2, vision and goals: talk to Claude, invite it to ask you questions until you have
  a solid vision. Clarify what success looks like and what would make the project
  particularly valuable or meaningful to you.
- Step 3, task breakdown and delegation analysis: identify major tasks; for each task,
  one at a time, discuss needed skills/knowledge/AI capabilities, which parts benefit
  from uniquely human strengths, which parts leverage AI well, and where collaboration
  has most impact. Produce a project plan with major tasks and delegation decisions.
- Save the plan: it is reused later for Description, Discernment, and Diligence practice.

Rules and traps
- Have a genuine conversation: challenge assumptions, ask for clarification, stay open to
  unexpected insights, rather than just exchanging statements.

Exercise and reflection: see Section 3.

### Lesson 8: A Closer Look at Description (video 4 min; "Lesson 6")

Objectives: understand how to communicate intentions to AI; recognize the importance of
clear, purposeful communication; develop skills in three types of Description.

Key ideas
- Description goes beyond writing prompts: it creates a collaborative environment where
  you and the AI can work effectively together.
- Three components (named exactly):
  - Product Description: clearly defining what you want the AI to create (outputs,
    format, audience, style).
  - Process Description: guiding how the AI approaches your request (can be as important
    as specifying the end goal).
  - Performance Description: defining how you want the AI to behave during the
    collaboration (for example concise or detailed, challenging or supportive).
- AI cannot read your mind; result quality often comes down to how clearly you articulate
  your needs, preferred approach, and desired interaction style.
- AI systems are interactive partners, not databases or vending machines.
- Clear communication up front saves time and leads to better results.

Rules and traps
- Treating AI as a database or vending machine is the misconception the lesson names.
- The most commonly overlooked component is a reflection question: which of product,
  process, or performance are you overlooking?

Exercise: Bad Prompt Makeover (see Section 3).

### Lesson 9: Effective Prompting Techniques (Deep Dive, "Lesson 7")

Objectives: understand what prompt engineering is and why it matters; apply six
foundational techniques; identify common patterns of successful interactions;
troubleshoot and refine prompts.

Key ideas
- Prompt engineering is simply the practice of designing effective instructions for AI
  systems, combining familiar human communication principles with AI-specific
  considerations.
- Six foundational techniques (named exactly as the course lists them):
  1. Give context: be specific about what you want, why you want it, and relevant
     background.
  2. Show examples: demonstrate the output style or format you are looking for.
  3. Specify constraints: clearly define format, length, and other output requirements.
  4. Break complex tasks into steps: guide the AI through multi-step reasoning.
  5. Ask the AI to think first: give space for the AI to work through its process.
  6. Define the AI's role or tone: specify how you want the AI to communicate.
- The "secret weapon": ask the AI itself to help improve your prompt.
- Successful prompting is iterative (and perhaps also collaborative with the AI); expect
  to refine your approach based on results.
- Common successful patterns: clear task overviews, format specifications, explicit
  constraints, and relevant background information.
- The video also shares troubleshooting strategies for when responses are not right.

Rules and traps
- Do not expect a perfect first prompt; iterate.
- Prompting skill is the practical side of the Description competency (a reflection
  prompt asks you to connect the two).

in the captured text.

### Lesson 10: A Closer Look at Discernment (video 5 min; "Lesson 8")

Objectives: evaluate AI outputs and processes thoughtfully; develop critical thinking in
AI interactions; identify and address quality concerns.

Key ideas
- Discernment is the competency of thoughtfully evaluating AI outputs, processes, and
  behaviors. It is the flip side of Description: Description communicates intentions,
  Discernment evaluates whether what you receive meets your needs.
- Three components (named exactly):
  - Product Discernment: evaluating the quality of AI outputs (accuracy,
    appropriateness, coherence, relevance).
  - Process Discernment: assessing how the AI approached the task, looking for logical
    errors, attention gaps, or inappropriate reasoning.
  - Performance Discernment: evaluating how the AI behaved during the interaction itself,
    including whether its communication style is effective for your needs.
- Discernment and Description work hand-in-hand in a continuous feedback loop.
- Even the most advanced AI systems benefit from human judgment and oversight.
- Together these skills keep AI collaboration guided by thoughtful human judgment.

Rules and traps
- Domain knowledge underpins Discernment: someone without your expertise may struggle to
  judge quality (the exercise's reflection step asks exactly this).

Exercise: Expert Discernment (see Section 3).

### Lesson 11: The Description-Discernment Loop (hands-on, 30-60 min)

Objectives: apply Description and Discernment to a real project; engage in productive
feedback loops; create results through human-AI collaboration that exceed what either
could achieve alone.

Key ideas: the loop for each task, as listed by the course
- Describe: be specific about what you want (Product); guide how Claude should approach
  or think about the task (Process); specify how you want Claude to engage with you
  (Performance).
- Discern: evaluate the output (Product Discernment); assess how Claude approached the
  task (Process Discernment); consider whether Claude's behavior is most helpful
  (Performance Discernment).
- Refine: give feedback on what worked and what did not; clarify or adjust your
  description; request iterations until satisfied.
- Integrate: add your own perspective, creativity, or domain knowledge; make the final
  decisions about what to keep, modify, or discard; take responsibility for the final
  output.

Rules and traps
- The human always makes final keep/modify/discard decisions and takes responsibility.
- Continue the loop for each task until the project is complete.

Exercise: Project execution with description-discernment loops (see Section 3).

### Lesson 12: A Closer Look at Diligence (video 7 min; "Lesson 10"; ~20 min)

Objectives: understand ethical implications of AI collaboration; understand the
importance of transparency; recognize your responsibility in AI interactions and outputs.

Key ideas
- Diligence is the competency for responsible and ethical AI collaboration: taking
  responsibility for our AI collaborations. The other competencies mainly address
  effectiveness and efficiency; Diligence addresses ethical and safety aspects that are
  equally crucial.
- Three components (named exactly):
  - Creation Diligence: being thoughtful about which AI systems you choose and how you
    work with them.
  - Transparency Diligence: being open (honest) about AI's role in your work with everyone
    who needs to know.
  - Deployment Diligence: taking ownership for AI-assisted outputs you share with others;
    verifying and vouching for the outputs you use or share.
- Different contexts (personal, academic, professional) may have different expectations
  for disclosure and verification; each of us is responsible for understanding and
  meeting them.
- Thoughtful Diligence makes AI collaboration not only effective and efficient but also
  ethical and safe.
- Diligence statement: a transparent acknowledgment of AI's role in your work, along with
  your commitment to responsibility for the final output. The course's example template
  (paraphrased): states which AI assistant you collaborated with and for which tasks;
  affirms that all AI-generated and co-created content was thoroughly reviewed and
  evaluated; says the final output reflects your understanding, expertise, and intended
  meaning; says you keep full responsibility for the content, its accuracy, and its
  presentation; and frames the disclosure as made in the spirit of transparency.

Rules and traps
- Using AI does not transfer responsibility: you remain accountable for the final output.
- Disclosure and verification norms depend on context; know your audience's expectations.

Exercise: Creating a Diligence Statement (see Section 3).

### Lesson 13: Conclusion (video 6 min; ~15 min)

Objectives: consolidate key learnings; connect AI Fluency to continuing development.

Key ideas (key takeaways as listed)
- AI Fluency develops through intentional practice of the four core competencies.
- Delegation: our expertise and judgment remain the foundation of effective AI
  collaboration.
- Description: clear communication that bridges our intentions and AI capabilities.
- Discernment: thoughtful and critical evaluation of outputs to work within the system's
  constraints.
- Diligence: ensures accountability, transparency, and responsibility in our AI work.
- The most powerful outcomes emerge when humans and AI build on each other's strengths.
- The framework is designed to remain relevant as AI systems evolve.
- The 4Ds apply across all three ways of interacting with AI (Automation, Augmentation,
  Agency). AI systems are powerful but not magical solutions; they are only as useful and
  safe as we enable them to be through thoughtful engagement.
- Closing message: approach AI as a thinking partner rather than just a tool; commit to
  effectiveness, efficiency, ethics, and safety; each interaction is a chance to refine
  skills.

## 3. Exercises, Quizzes and Reflection Prompts

### Lesson 1

- Putting things into practice (5-10 min): get access to a language model (claude.ai
  free account, or another chatbot). Tests: readiness only; no evaluation.
- Reflection: challenges you have met working with AI to achieve specific outcomes;

### Lesson 3 (4D Framework)

- Exercise 1: Apply the 4Ds (5 min). Pick one scenario and answer one question per D.
  - Communication project (draft a series of marketing-campaign emails):
    Delegation - what you handle vs. collaborate on; Description - how to communicate
    tone, purpose, success criteria; Discernment - criteria to judge the drafts;
    Diligence - transparency and responsibility considerations.
  - Research project (analyze a large dataset for a research paper):
    Delegation - divide analytical work; Description - what context AI needs about the
    research question; Discernment - verify the analysis for accuracy; Diligence -
    ethical considerations when publishing AI-assisted research.
  - Creative project (character concepts for a story):
    Delegation - which creative elements to explore with AI vs. develop independently;
    Description - guide AI to fit the story's world; Discernment - decide what to keep,
    modify, or discard; Diligence - how to acknowledge AI's contribution.
  - Tests: mapping each D to a concrete task. The course gives no model answers.
- Exercise 2: Explore something you love (5-10 min). Chat with Claude about a topic you
  know well. Notice moments where Claude enhances your thinking; where you must clarify
  or correct Claude's understanding; where your expertise leads you to evaluate its
  responses. Tests: seeing Discernment grounded in domain expertise. Trying several AI
  assistants is suggested to see how they differ.
- Exercise 3: Learn something new (5-10 min). Ask Claude to teach a topic you do not
  know. Do not worry about prompting "wrong" or "right". Notice where Claude offers
  helpful explanations, gives concrete examples, responds naturally to questions, and
  explains things you would want to double check. Tests: contrast with Exercise 2 (the
  need to verify when you lack expertise).
- Reflection: which D you feel most confident in and which needs development; a recent AI
  interaction the framework might have helped; which 4D skills would most enhance your
  work or personal projects.

### Lesson 6 (Delegation)

- Exercise: Analyze tasks with an AI assistant. Pick a small task (email, presentation

  each side may see something the other does not. Then create a simple delegation plan
  that uses both parties' strengths. Tests: Problem Awareness and Task Delegation.
- Reflection: how a recent AI project would differ with the Delegation framework in mind;
  which kinds of tasks in your work or studies benefit most from AI collaboration.

### Lesson 7 (Project planning)

- Three-step project exercise (see Lesson 7 above). Tests: applying Delegation to a
  multi-step project; the saved plan feeds later lessons.
- Reflection: insights from the planning conversation; the most challenging aspect of the
  delegation plan; what additional information or skills would help you delegate better.

### Lesson 8 (Description)

- Exercise: Bad Prompt Makeover (10 min). Ask Claude to challenge you with poorly written
  prompts; improve each using Product (what exactly you want), Process (how Claude should
  approach it), and Performance (how Claude should behave) thinking. Discuss before/after
  versions and ask Claude how your improvements help it respond. After about 5 minutes,
  switch roles: give Claude bad prompts to fix and notice what information it adds and
  how it organizes it. Tests: the three types of Description.
- Reflection: which Description component (product, process, performance) you may be
  overlooking; how better description might have improved a recent unsatisfying
  interaction.

### Lesson 9 (Prompting)

- Reflection: which of the six techniques would most enhance your interactions; which
  techniques might have improved a recent poor interaction; how the techniques connect to
  the Description competency. Optional: revisit Bad Prompt Makeover to practice the six
  principles.

### Lesson 10 (Discernment)

- Exercise: Expert Discernment, evaluating AI responses in your domain. Goal: practice
  Product, Process and Performance Discernment where you have expertise.
  - Step 1: return to your topic from Lesson 3 Exercise 2 ("Explore something you love").
  - Step 2: in a new conversation, ask Claude for three different explanations or
    analyses of a specific aspect (examples: three explanations of depth of field for
    photography; three analyses of fermentation techniques for cooking; three
    perspectives on a historical event).
  - Step 3: evaluate each explanation.
    - Product: which is most accurate; factual errors or misconceptions; is the level of
      detail appropriate for a learner.
    - Process: logical reasoning; gaps in the analysis or thinking; appropriate
      connections between concepts.
    - Performance: attentiveness to your question and responsiveness to feedback and
      direction; appropriate terminology; effect of tone and style on clarity.
  - Step 4: feedback and refinement. Name the strongest explanation and say specifically
    why it works; name the weakest and give specific feedback on its problems; work with
    Claude to produce an improved version.
  - Step 5: reflection with Claude: what specific knowledge let you spot strengths and
    weaknesses; how someone without your expertise might struggle; what this shows about
    the relationship between domain knowledge and effective Discernment.
  - A more playful alternative: "Game Night" in Additional Activities.
- Reflection: which type of Discernment is most challenging and why; how Discernment
  complements Description; what signals or patterns indicate an output needs closer
  scrutiny.

### Lesson 11 (Description-Discernment loop, 30-60 min)

- Exercise: project execution. Step 1 review the project plan (refine if needed).
  Step 2 prepare the Description approach with Claude: Product (outputs, format, style,
  length, level of detail), Process (methods, frameworks, steps), Performance (concise or
  detailed, challenging or supportive, ideas or analysis). Step 3 execute each task with
  the loop Describe -> Discern -> Refine -> Integrate. Tests: combining Description and
  Discernment on real work; human integration and responsibility.
- Reflection: patterns in descriptions that led to the best outcomes; whether Description
  or Discernment took more effort and why; how execution compared to the initial plan and
  what adjustments were made.

### Lesson 12 (Diligence)

- Exercise: Creating a Diligence Statement (about 14 min).
  - Step 1 (3 min): understand diligence statements (the example template above).
  - Step 2 (5 min): reflect on the collaboration.
    - Creation Diligence: which AI systems you chose and why; what data or information
      you shared; privacy, security, or ethical considerations in your choices.
    - Transparency Diligence: the audience for the output; their expectations on AI
      disclosure; how specifically AI contributed to different aspects of the work.
    - Deployment Diligence: steps taken to verify accuracy and appropriateness; how you
      ensured the final output meets your standards; what responsibility you take for the
      final product.
  - Step 3 (6 min): draft the statement with Claude, sharing your reflections and
    optionally past conversations. It should address: which AI systems you used; how AI
    contributed; the review process; your assertion of responsibility for the final
    output; any context-specific considerations (academic, professional, etc.).
  - Step 4: add the statement to your finished project (for example footer, appendix, or
    metadata).
  - The page also says the diligence statement for the course itself is provided.
- Reflection: which of Creation, Transparency, Deployment is most challenging; how
  Diligence varies by context (personal, academic, professional); how acknowledging AI's
  role affects how others perceive the work; unanticipated ethical considerations; what
  personal guidelines you might develop.

### Lesson 13 (Conclusion)

- Exercise: Discuss the AI Fluency course with Claude (optionally upload a copy of the
  framework; the page gives a ringling.libguides.com link). Conversation starters:
  apply the 4Ds to your degree program or job; how the framework might evolve as AI
  advances and what new competencies might become important; which of the 4Ds you seem
  to understand well and which to develop; analyze an exercise you did to improve your
  competencies.
- Exercise: Build a Personal AI Policy with Claude as thinking partner. Elements:
  - Clear standards for when and how you will work with AI in different contexts.
  - Boundaries for sensitive or confidential information.
  - How you will maintain quality control for AI-assisted work.
  - Ethical issues most relevant to your field or activities.
  - Decision-making criteria for ethical dilemmas.
  - Perspectives of different people affected by your AI interactions.
  - How you will disclose AI collaboration in different contexts.
  - Templates for attribution and transparency statements.
  - Criteria for when more detailed disclosure is appropriate.
- Reflection: the two exercises serve as the reflection for this lesson.

### Lesson 15: Additional Activities (self-paced)

- Build a personal AI fluency plan. Parts:
  - Current competency assessment: rate each 4D competency (novice, developing,
    confident); document strengths and growth areas; identify which ways of interacting
    (Automation, Augmentation, Agency) you use most and least effectively.
  - Development priority identification: pick 1-2 competencies first; identify valuable
    aspects in your context; consider which interaction ways to become more proficient
    with.
  - Specific action planning: activities per priority competency; timeline and frequency
    of practice; resources and tools; how you will recognize progress.
- Build a personal prompt and pattern library. Parts:
  - Common task template creation: identify 5-10 tasks you regularly do with AI; develop
    a template prompt for each; include placeholders for variable information.
  - Effective strategy documentation: Description techniques per task type; Discernment
    patterns; which approaches suit which interaction modes.
  - Personal reference resource building: organize for easy search; include examples of
    successful outputs; a system for updating the library.
  - You can analyze past successful interactions with Claude to find patterns.
- Game Night (practice precise Description and sharpen Discernment via word puzzles;
  puzzles have specific instructions, so they suit clear communication, and they are
  deliberately tricky, so they give Discernment workouts):
  - Swap Riddles: create a riddle (example given: "Five-score and more drums I tap to
    paint a symphony of light. What am I doing?" Answer: typing on a computer keyboard,
    usually 104 keys, to create pixels on a monitor). Have Claude guess and explain its
    reasoning; note why, not just what. Do not give the answer; nudge toward the right
    chain of thought and context. Then switch sides.
  - Co-operative crosswords: work on clues together (cryptic clues especially
    interesting). Focus is on guiding and refining thinking, not on solving skill.
    Example steering: "it's only 5 letters", "can't be that because it starts with B",
    "maybe they mean swallow like the bird". These steering skills carry over to real
    projects.
  - Word association: pick 12-20 random words and find relationships with Claude; point
    out questionable connections and overlooked patterns; be open to connections Claude
    spots that you miss. Practice all three types of Discernment:
    - Product Discernment: do answers make logical sense and fit a cohesive theme?
    - Process Discernment: how does Claude approach the puzzle; does it consider multiple
      possibilities or focus too narrowly on one interpretation?
    - Performance Discernment: how clearly does Claude communicate its thinking; does it
      ask good questions; does it build on your suggestions?
  - More game suggestions: Twenty Questions (swap roles; notice how each formulates
    questions and clues); Collaborative Storytelling (alternate sentences; observe
    narrative coherence); A Wizard Approaches You in the Tavern (role-playing game with
    the model as player or game master); Concepts & Constraints (a complex concept with
    tight constraints such as "explain using cooking metaphors only"; compare
    approaches); Advanced Puzzling (create logic or word puzzles for Claude, or have
    Claude create them; programmers may tackle problems such as Project Euler).

## 4. Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---------|-----|
| Treating AI Fluency as memorizing best prompts or becoming an AI technical expert | Define it as working with AI effectively, efficiently, ethically, safely, via the 4Ds |
| Trying to automate everything | Decide deliberately what to do yourself, with AI, or leave to AI (Delegation) |
| Involving AI before knowing the goal | Build Problem Awareness first: goals and the work needed |
| Ignoring differences between AI platforms | Build Platform Awareness: capabilities and limitations of each system |
| Delegating without domain expertise or AI knowledge | Delegation needs both field expertise and understanding of AI capabilities |
| Expecting AI to read your mind | Use Description: say what you want, how, and how to behave |
| Treating AI as a database or vending machine | Treat it as an interactive partner; describe the collaboration |
| Describing only the product | Add Process Description (approach) and Performance Description (behavior) |
| Expecting a perfect first prompt | Iterate, refine, and optionally ask the AI to help improve the prompt |
| Accepting output without evaluation | Apply Product, Process and Performance Discernment; humans keep oversight |
| Judging only the final output | Also assess how the AI reasoned (Process) and how it behaved (Performance) |
| Relying on AI where you lack expertise without checking | Double check explanations; domain knowledge enables Discernment |
| Ignoring known limitations | Account for knowledge cutoffs, hallucinations, context window limits, reasoning challenges |
| Not disclosing AI's role | Practice Transparency Diligence; add a diligence statement; know context expectations |
| Passing off AI output as vetted without verifying | Deployment Diligence: verify and vouch for what you share |
| Assuming AI involvement shifts accountability | You remain responsible for the final output |
| Expecting fluency overnight | It develops through intentional, repeated practice |

|------|---------|
| AI Fluency | Ability to collaborate with AI in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical, and safe |
| AI Fluency Framework | Framework by Dakan and Feller centered on the 4Ds |
| 4Ds | Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence |
| Automation | AI completes specific tasks based on your instructions |
| Augmentation | You and AI collaborate as creative thinking and task execution partners |

| Platform Awareness | Knowing what different AI systems can do (capabilities and limitations) |

| Process Discernment | Assessing how the AI arrived at the output: logical errors, attention gaps, inappropriate reasoning |

| Diligence | Responsible and ethical AI collaboration; taking responsibility |

## 6. Exam-style Self-test

1. Q: What does AI Fluency mean?
   A: The ability to collaborate with AI in ways that are effective, efficient, ethical,
   and safe.

2. Q: Name the 4Ds.
   A: Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence.

3. Q: Name the three ways of engaging with AI and distinguish them.
   A: Automation (AI does specific tasks on your instructions); Augmentation (you and AI
   collaborate as thinking and execution partners); Agency (you configure AI to work
   independently, setting its knowledge and behavior).

4. Q: Which competency decides what work to do with AI vs. yourself, and what are its
   three components?
   A: Delegation: Problem Awareness, Platform Awareness, Task Delegation.

5. Q: What is Problem Awareness, and why does it come before involving AI?
   A: Clearly understanding your goals and the nature of the work needed, so you can
   decide sensibly how to involve AI.

6. Q: What are the three components of Description?
   A: Product (what to create), Process (how to approach it), Performance (how the AI
   should behave during collaboration).

7. Q: List the six foundational prompting techniques.
   A: Give context; show examples; specify constraints; break complex tasks into steps;
   ask the AI to think first; define the AI's role or tone.

8. Q: What is the "secret weapon" of prompting?
   A: Asking the AI itself to help improve your prompt.

9. Q: Distinguish Product, Process and Performance Discernment.
   A: Product evaluates output quality; Process evaluates how the AI reasoned or approached
   the task; Performance evaluates how the AI behaved in the interaction.

10. Q: How do Description and Discernment relate?
    A: Discernment is the flip side of Description; together they form a continuous
    feedback loop.

11. Q: What are the steps of the loop used in the project exercise?
    A: Describe, Discern, Refine, Integrate (the human integrates expertise, makes final
    decisions, and takes responsibility).

12. Q: Name the three components of Diligence.
    A: Creation Diligence, Transparency Diligence, Deployment Diligence.

13. Q: What does Deployment Diligence require?
    A: Taking responsibility for verifying and vouching for the outputs you use or share.

14. Q: Which two learning stages do generative AI models go through, and what does each do?
    A: Pre-training (analyzing patterns across billions of examples) and fine-tuning
    (learning to follow instructions and give helpful responses).

15. Q: Name the current limitations of generative AI listed in the course.
    A: Knowledge cutoff dates, hallucinations, context window constraints, and challenges
    with complex reasoning.
